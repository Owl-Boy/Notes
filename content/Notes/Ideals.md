# Ideals
---
In all concrete categories, the idea of quotients is very close to the idea of surjections on the underlying set. In a lot of algebraic structures like groups and rings, because of the structure imposed by the operations.

In case of groups, an example would be that the kernel of any quotient map must be a normal sub group.

Let consider the kernel of any subgroup:
- One important property of a $0$ is a ring is that $0\cdot r=0$ for any element $r$ of the ring.

This suggests the following definition for the following property which is necessarily true for the kernel of a map:
>[!definition]
>Let $R$ be a ring. A subgroup $I$ of $(R, +)$ is an **Ideal** of $R$ if for any $r$ we have 
>$$
>r \cdot I \subseteq I \supseteq I \cdot r
>$$
>We can similarly define left and right ideals.

>[!note]
>**Ideals** are usually not rings, given a non-zero ideal $R$, if we want an **ideal** to be a sub-ring, then it must include $1_{R}$, but that would force $I$ to be $R$ itself.
