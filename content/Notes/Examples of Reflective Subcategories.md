# Examples of Reflective Subcategories
---
>[!example]
>Compact Hausdorff spaces define a reflective subcategory $\text{cHaus}\hookrightarrow\text{Top}$ of the category of all topological spaces. The reflector $\beta:\text{Top}\to\text{cHaus}$ is the functor that sends a space to its [[Stone Cech Compactification]]. The universal property of the unit says that any continuous function $X\to K$ from a space $X$ to a compact Hasudorff space $K$ extends uniquely to the Stone-Cech Compactification.

>[!example]
>Abelian groups define a reflective subcategory $\text{Ab}\hookrightarrow\text{Group}$ of the category of groups. The reflector carries a group $G$ to its [[Abelianization|abelianization]], the quotient $G /[G,G]$ by the commutator subgroup, the normal subgroup generated by element $ghg^{-1}h^{-1}$. The quotient map defines the components of the unit. For any abelian group $A$, there is an isomorphism $A \cong A/[A, A]$; the commutator subgroup is trivial iff a group $G$ is abelian. The Adjunction asserts that a homomorphism from a group $G$ to an abelian group necessarily factors through the abelianization of $G$.

>[!example]
>The inclusion $\text{Ab}_{\text{tf}}\hookrightarrow\text{Ab}$ of torsion-free abelian groups is reflective and the reflector quotients a group by its torsion subgroup. This states that any map to a torsion-free abelian group must have the torsion of elements of $A$ contained in its kernel.
