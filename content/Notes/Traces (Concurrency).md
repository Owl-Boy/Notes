# Trace
---
An execution of the concurrent system can be through of by a sequence of events ordered by time. These are represented by words, which represent the events in a linear order, decided by when the events were executed. A single execution, always guarantees the acceptance of all words that are created by swapping any adjacent letters which are independent (not shared by any process). Thus suggests that linear orders are too strict to model runs of a concurrent systems. By removing the ordering between independent events, we get a *Trace*, which is a partial order, such that any linearization of the trace should be accepted by the concurrent system.

Unlike [[Distributed Alphabet|distributed alphabets]], traces are not dependent on the precise set of process, but only on the dependence relation on different events

This dependence relation is reflexive and symmetric, making the independence relation irreflexive and symmetric.

This independence relation induces the following relation on the set of words:
$$
u\ a\ b\ v \sim' u\ b\ a\ v, \text{ if } (a, b)\in \mathcal I 
$$
where $u\in \Sigma^*$ and $\mathcal I$ is the independence relation. Let $\sim$ be the equivalence relation generated by this.

This equivalence relation is a congruence under concatenation, that is: $[w] \cdot [w'] = [ww']$. This is also both left and right cancellable.

Each equivalence class is called a **Trace** and the set $\Sigma / \sim$ is called the set of traces.

> [!EXAMPLE] 
> Consider the alphabet $\Sigma = \{a,b,c,d\}$ and the independence relation $\mathcal I = \{(a, c), (b, d)\}$, then, consider the word $bdabcbadb$, then the trace corresponding to the word consists of all of the following:
> - $bdabcbadb$
> - $bdabcbabd$
> - $dbabcbadb$
> - $dbabcbabd$
>
> And is represented by the following notation:
> $$
> \begin{matrix}
> b &&&&&&&&&&&&&& d\\
> & \diagdown &&&&&&&&&&&&\diagup\\ 
> && c &- & a & - & b & - & c & - & b & - & a \\
> & \diagup   &&&&&&&&&&&&\diagdown\\
> d &&&&&&&&&&&&&& b
> \end{matrix}
> $$

A language that is closed under the above relation is called *trace closed*, and all concurrent systems only accept trace closed languages. One can also think of concurrent systems as those which accept a set of trances rather than a set of words.
