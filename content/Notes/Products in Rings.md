# Products in Rings
---
Given rings $R_{1}$ and $R_{2}$, one can construct the ring $R_{1} \times R_{2}$ in the following way:
- Let the underlying set be the product of underlying sets
- Let addition and multiplication be component-wise
- Let the multiplicative identity element be $(1_{R_{1}}, 1_{R_{2}})$
- Let the additive identity be $(0_{R_{1}}, 0_{R_{2}})$

This satisfies the properties of the [[Products and Coporducts]]. Given any ring $S$ with morphisms $\varphi_{1}:S \to R_{1}$ and $\varphi_{2}:S \to R_{2}$, there is a unique morphism $\varphi:S \to R_{1}\times R_{2}$ which is $(\varphi_{1}, \varphi_{2})$ which commutes with the projections.
