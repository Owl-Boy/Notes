# Krull Dimension
---
>[!definition]
>The **Krull Dimension** of a commutative ring $R$ is the length of the longest chain of Prime Ideals

>[!example]
>In case of PIDs, like $\mathbb{Z}$ or $\mathbb{C}[x]$, the Krull Dimensions is always $1$ because all prime ideals are maximal.

>[!example]
>Consider the ring 
>$$
>K[x_{1},x_{2},\dots x_{n}]
>$$
>where $K$ is a field. Then there is a chain of prime ideals:
>$$
>(0) \subseteq (x_{1}) \subseteq (x_{1},x_{2}) \subseteq \dots \subseteq (x_{1},x_{2}\dots x_{n})
>$$
>Which implies that the **Krull Dimension** is at least $n$. Turns out that it is exactly $n$ but apparently that is a hard result to prove.
