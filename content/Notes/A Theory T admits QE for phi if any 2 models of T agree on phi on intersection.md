# A Theory T admits Quantifier Elimination for phi if any 2 models of T agree on phi on intersection

---

> [!THM]
> Given the following information:
> - $\mathcal L$ : a theory.
> - $c : \mathcal L$ : A constant.
> - $\mathcal M, \mathcal N$ : models such that $\mathcal M \models T$ and $\mathcal N \models T$
> - $\phi(\bar v)$
> - $A$ : common substructure of $\mathcal M$ and $\mathcal N$.
> - $\bar a$ : a subset of $A$
>
> The following are equivalent:
> - There is a quantifier free $\mathcal L$-formula $\psi(\bar v)$ such that $T \models \forall \bar x, (\phi(\bar x) \leftrightarrow \psi(\bar x))$.
> - $\mathcal M \models \phi(\bar a)$ iff $\mathcal N \models \psi(\bar a)$

First we show the forward direction, the first and last step use the hypothesis:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal M \models \phi(\bar a) & \Leftrightarrow \mathcal M \models \psi(\bar a) \\
& \Leftrightarrow A \models \psi(\bar a) \\
& \Leftrightarrow \mathcal N \models \psi(\bar a) \\
& \Leftrightarrow \mathcal N \models \phi(\bar a)
\end{aligned}
$$

and we are done.

For the other direction, we first solve the trivial cases:
- $T\models \forall \bar v, \phi(\bar v)$, then we can give the formula $c=c$
- $T\models \forall \bar v,\lnot \phi(\bar v)$, then we can give the formula $c\ne c$

If the above two are not the case, then we can safely assume that both $\forall \bar v, \phi(\bar v)$ and $\forall \bar v, \phi(\bar v)$ are consistent with $T$.

Let
$$
\Gamma(\bar v) = \{\psi(\bar v) : \psi(\bar v) \text{ is quantifier free and }T\models \forall \bar v, (\phi(\bar v) \rightarrow \psi(\bar v))\}
$$

We can now add constants $\bar d$, and we will show that $T\cup \Gamma(\bar d) \models \phi(\bar d)$. Now by [[Compactness Theorem]], we get a finite subset of $\Gamma(\bar d)$ that satisfies $\phi$

Thus we get the following:
$$
T\models \forall \bar d, \left(\bigwedge \psi_n(\bar d) \leftrightarrow \phi(\bar d)\right)
$$

Thus we are now left to show that $T\cup \Gamma(\bar d) \models \phi(\bar d)$.

Suppose not, then $T\cup\Gamma(\bar d) \cup \{ \lnot\phi(\bar d)\}$ is satisfied by a model $\mathcal M$ and let $\mathcal A$ be the substructure of $\mathcal M$ generated by $\bar d$.

Let $\Sigma = T \cup \text{Diag}(\mathcal A) \cup \{ \phi(\bar d)\}$. If $\Sigma$ is not satisfiable, then we can find a finite subset of $\text{Diag}(\mathcal A)$ such that

$$
T\models \forall \bar v \left( \bigwedge \psi(\bar v) \to \lnot \phi(\bar v) \right)
$$

And taking the contrapositive we get

$$
T\models \forall \bar v \left( \phi(\bar v) \to \bigvee \lnot\psi(\bar v) \right)
$$

So we get that $\bigvee \lnot \psi(\bar v) \in \Gamma$ and $A\models \bigvee \lnot\psi(\bar v)$, which is a contradiction. Thus $\Sigma$ is satisfiable.

Let $\mathcal N$ be a model of $\Sigma$ then $\mathcal N \models \psi(\bar d)$. But $A \subseteq N$, this contradicts the hypothesis.
